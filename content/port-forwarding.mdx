## Optional router route

Port forwarding tells the router to send one incoming internet port to the Minecraft server’s private LAN address. Router screens differ; use the manufacturer or ISP’s current instructions for the exact buttons.

> [!DANGER] Forward only Minecraft
> Do not expose the ZimaOS dashboard, Crafty dashboard, SSH, databases, or Docker management ports. The only rule in this guide is Minecraft TCP 25565.

1. Confirm the server’s LAN IP is reserved.
2. Open the router app or administrator page.
3. Find **Port Forwarding**, **NAT**, **Virtual Server**, or **Applications & Gaming**.
4. Create a TCP rule for external port `25565`.
5. Choose the ZimaOS server or enter its reserved LAN IP.
6. Set internal port `25565`.
7. Save/apply.
8. Test from cellular data or another home network.

## Terms that explain failures

A **private IP** such as `192.168.x.x` works only inside home. A **public IP** identifies the internet-facing connection. **TCP** is Minecraft Java’s main reliable transport. External port is the public door; internal port is Minecraft’s local door.

**Double NAT** means two routers both perform routing, so both may need correct configuration or bridge mode. **CGNAT** means the ISP shares a public address; ordinary inbound forwarding usually cannot reach your router. A tunnel such as Playit is often simpler. A dynamic public IP can change later.

Testing your own public address from the same Wi-Fi may fail when the router lacks **NAT loopback**, even though an outside friend can join.

Sharing a public IP with trusted friends is common, but it reveals the approximate ISP/network location. Do not post it publicly. Use a hostname or tunnel when you need easier sharing—not as a claim of perfect anonymity or security.
