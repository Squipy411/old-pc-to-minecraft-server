## TPS and MSPT without the math fog

Minecraft aims for **20 ticks per second (TPS)**. Each tick updates mobs, redstone, weather, and more. At 20 TPS, a tick has a **50 millisecond** budget. **MSPT** is milliseconds per tick. Under 50 MSPT can sustain 20 TPS; consistently above 50 means the world starts running in slow motion.

Ping is network travel time. A player can have high ping while TPS remains perfect, or low ping while the whole server lags. Ask whether everyone feels the slowdown.

## What usually costs time

New chunk generation, many spread-out players, entities, item piles, mob farms, hoppers, redstone clocks, large view/simulation distances, and incompatible mods increase CPU work. An HDD can stall chunk loads and saves. Too little RAM causes pressure; too much assigned heap is not a CPU upgrade.

## A calm lag checklist

1. Ask whether the lag affects everyone or one player.
2. Check TPS/MSPT in the server/mod metrics.
3. Check Crafty/ZimaOS RAM use and whether swapping occurs.
4. Check CPU use; one saturated core can be the limit.
5. Check disk activity and free space.
6. Remove or roll back only the newly added mod batch.
7. Reduce `view-distance` to 8 and `simulation-distance` to 6 as a test.
8. Pregenerate a bounded area before exploration sessions.
9. Inspect huge farms, entity buildup, and always-on redstone.
10. Make a backup, then restart normally.

> [!WARNING] JVM flags are not a magic spell
> Use the server software/modpack’s documented Java options. Copying an unexplained flag collection can hurt compatibility or hide the real bottleneck.

Optimization mods such as Lithium or FerriteCore can help when their exact release supports your Minecraft/Fabric combination. Measure before and after one change.
